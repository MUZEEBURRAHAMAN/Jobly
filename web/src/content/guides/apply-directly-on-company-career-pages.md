Big job sites make applying quick: one click, your saved profile, done. That speed is useful, but where you apply can change what happens next.

## What happens when you apply

Most tech companies use an applicant tracking system (ATS) such as Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or Workday. Recruiters review candidates inside that system.

- **On the careers page,** your application goes straight into the ATS, in the same format as everyone else's, with every question the team asked.
- **Through a job site's own apply button,** your application may be sent across as a profile, a PDF or an email, depending on how the employer set it up. Some teams handle these the same way; others review them later or less carefully.

You can't see how a given company handles it, so applying directly is the safer default for roles you care about.

## Other reasons to go direct

- **The listing is current.** Careers pages show jobs while they're open. Copies elsewhere can linger after the role is filled.
- **You see the full posting,** including team details, pay range and application questions that aggregators sometimes cut.
- **You can answer the custom questions.** "Why this company?" and portfolio links help a recruiter, and they're often skipped in one-click flows.

## How to find the careers page

1. Search the company name plus "careers" or "jobs".
2. Look for addresses such as `boards.greenhouse.io/…`, `jobs.lever.co/…`, `jobs.ashbyhq.com/…` or `….myworkdayjobs.com`. These are the employer's own ATS.
3. Find the same job title. If it's not there, the role may be closed.

## When the quick button is fine

For a long list of similar roles, one-click apply can be a reasonable way to cast a wide net. Save your effort for the roles you'd really want, and apply to those directly.

## Common questions

### Does applying directly guarantee a reply?

No. It puts your application in the employer's own system, complete and in the expected format. What happens next still depends on fit, timing and how many people applied.

### Should I apply both ways to be safe?

Usually not. Duplicate applications can look careless, and the ATS often merges them anyway. Pick the employer's own site.

### What if the careers page asks me to create an account?

Some systems, Workday in particular, ask for an account per company. Use a password manager and a dedicated job-search email address so this stays manageable.
