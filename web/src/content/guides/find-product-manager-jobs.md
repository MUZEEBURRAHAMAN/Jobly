Product management roles get far more applicants than most tech jobs, and the title means different things at different companies. A focused search beats a broad one.

## Know the titles and levels

- **Associate product manager (APM):** entry level, often a formal programme.
- **Product manager:** the core role.
- **Senior, group or principal product manager:** more scope, or managing other PMs.
- **Technical product manager:** works on platforms, APIs or infrastructure, and usually needs an engineering background.
- **Product lead, head of product, director of product:** leadership roles, common at startups.

## Pick a lane

Recruiters look for evidence you've worked on similar problems. Decide which kinds of roles you're strongest for:

- **Product type:** consumer, B2B, developer tools, platform, growth.
- **Industry:** fintech, health, AI, e-commerce.
- **Company stage:** early startup, scale-up, large company.

Then apply mostly inside that lane. Five well-matched applications usually do better than fifty generic ones.

## Where PM jobs are posted

- **Company careers pages,** first and in full.
- **LinkedIn and Indeed,** with the date filter on.
- **Product communities and newsletters,** for roles shared by people who know the team.

## Make your application specific

- **Lead with outcomes:** "Raised activation from 31% to 44%" says more than "owned onboarding".
- **Match the job's language.** If it says "B2B SaaS" and "pricing", show your work on exactly that.
- **Keep it short.** A clear one-page résumé is a good sign for a PM.
- **Use the custom questions.** "Why this product?" is your best chance to show product thinking.

## Apply early

Popular PM roles can get hundreds of applicants in the first days. Seeing the job on day one, with Jobly's alerts or a careful date filter, gives your application a better chance of being read.

## Common questions

### Can I become a PM without PM experience?

Yes, but usually through a side door: an APM programme, or moving into product from a role next to it, such as engineering, design, data, customer success or consulting. Show the product decisions you already made in that role.

### Should I apply to "technical product manager" roles?

If you have an engineering background or have worked closely on APIs and infrastructure, yes. These roles often get fewer applicants than general PM roles. If you haven't, the interview will likely expose it.

### How many applications should I send?

There's no magic number. A good rhythm is a few well-matched applications each week, sent within a day or two of the job going up, plus time for networking with people at the companies you like most.
