A ghost job is a listing for a role nobody is actively hiring for. The job may already be filled, on hold, or posted only to collect résumés. Applying costs you time and hope, and you never hear back.

You can't always tell from the outside, but a few checks catch most of them.

## Warning signs

- **It's old.** A job that has been up for more than a month or two is less likely to be actively hiring. Many sites hide the real age: Indeed, for example, shows "30+ days" for anything older.
- **It keeps coming back.** The same job reposted every few weeks can mean the team keeps collecting applications without hiring.
- **It's vague.** No team, no manager, no clear responsibilities, and requirements that could fit any company.
- **There's no pay range where the law requires one.** Several US states, including California, Colorado, New York and Washington, require a pay range in job posts. A listing without one in those places is a small red flag.
- **It isn't on the company's own site.** If you can't find the job on the employer's careers page, it may have been taken down there and copied elsewhere.
- **"Always hiring" or "talent pool".** Some listings say openly that they're for future roles. That's honest, but don't spend an hour on the application.

## A two-minute check

1. **Find the job on the employer's careers page.** Search the company name plus "careers". If the job isn't there, be cautious.
2. **Look for the original posting date.** Careers pages built on Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby show jobs only while they’re open, and some show when the job went up.
3. **Check the team.** On LinkedIn, see whether the company has recently hired people with that title, or whether the hiring manager is posting about the role.
4. **Ask.** A short message to a recruiter, "Is this role still open?", often gets an answer.

## Don't take it personally

Ghost jobs are one reason a search can feel like shouting into a void. If you apply mostly to jobs that are recent and listed on the employer's own site, more of your applications reach a real person, and the silence from the rest says less about you.
