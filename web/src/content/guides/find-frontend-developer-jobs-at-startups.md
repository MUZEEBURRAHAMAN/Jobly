Startups are a good place for frontend developers: you ship to real users quickly and often own a whole part of the product. But small companies don't pay to advertise on every job site, so their jobs are easy to miss.

## Where startups post

- **Their own careers page.** Many startups use Ashby, Lever or Greenhouse. Jobs go up there first, sometimes only there.
- **Startup job boards.** Boards run by investors and accelerators list roles at the companies they back.
- **Founders and engineers on social media.** Early hires are often announced in a post before a formal listing exists.

Jobly reads hundreds of these careers pages, including many startups, and shows new frontend jobs within a few hours of going up.

## Search for the right titles

Frontend roles go by many names:

- Frontend engineer, front-end developer, UI engineer
- Web engineer, web developer
- Software engineer, frontend or software engineer, web
- React engineer, or roles that name JavaScript or TypeScript
- Design engineer, for roles between design and code

Jobly's **Frontend** role covers these. At startups, also look at **Fullstack** and **Product engineer** roles: many are mostly frontend work.

## What startups look for

- **Shipping.** Links to things you built and launched say more than a list of frameworks.
- **Range.** Small teams want someone who can handle an API change, a CSS bug and a design question in one day.
- **Product sense.** Mention a decision you made because of users, not only how you built it.
- **Care for quality:** accessibility, performance and a tidy UI stand out.

## Check the startup before you apply

- How long has it been around, and when did it last raise money?
- How big is the team? Your first weeks look very different at 8 people and at 80.
- Is the role new or a replacement? Both are fine, but ask in the first call.

## Move quickly

Small teams often hire from the first good candidates they talk to. Apply within a day or two of a job going up, and keep your application short and specific.

## Common questions

### Do startups pay less?

Often in cash, sometimes not. Many offer equity to make up the difference. Ask for the salary range early, and treat equity at an early startup as a bonus, not as salary.

### Is React required?

Not always, but it's the most common frontend framework in startup job posts. If you know another framework well, say so; the skills transfer, and many teams know it.

### How do I stand out without startup experience?

Show you can own something end to end: a side project with real users, an open-source contribution, or a feature you shipped from idea to launch at a larger company.
