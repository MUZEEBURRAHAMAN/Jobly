Remote design jobs get a lot of applicants, because anyone in the allowed region can apply. That makes two things matter more than usual: finding the job early, and showing quickly that you fit.

## Search for every title designers go by

The same job is posted under different names. Search for each:

- Product designer, UX designer, UI/UX designer
- Interaction designer, user experience designer
- Design systems designer, design engineer, design technologist
- Visual designer and brand designer, if your work leans that way

Jobly groups all of these under **Product design**, so one preference covers them.

## Where remote design jobs are posted

- **Company career pages.** Product-led companies often hire designers directly through Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby. Jobs appear there first.
- **Remote job boards.** We Work Remotely, Remotive and Himalayas list only remote jobs. Jobly reads these too.
- **Design communities.** Job boards run by design communities and newsletters often carry roles that never reach the big sites.

## Read the location fine print

"Remote" rarely means "anywhere". Before applying, check for:

- **A country or region:** "Remote (US)", "Remote, EMEA".
- **Time-zone overlap:** "4 hours overlap with Eastern Time".
- **Travel:** some remote teams meet in person a few times a year.
- **Where the employer can hire.** Many companies can only employ people in countries where they have a legal entity.

Turn on **Remote only** in Jobly to hide on-site and hybrid roles, and add your region as a keyword if you only want jobs open to it.

## Make your application quick to say yes to

- **Lead with two or three case studies** that match the company's product: B2B tools, consumer apps, design systems.
- **Show the outcome,** not only the screens: what changed for users or the business.
- **Write for async work.** Remote teams read more than they meet. A clear, short written case study shows you'll fit.
- **Make the portfolio fast.** A reviewer may give it two minutes. Put the best project first.

## A weekly routine

1. Set Jobly to Product design, your experience level and Remote only.
2. Check new matches each morning, and apply to the best ones the same day.
3. Spend the time you save on one great application rather than five rushed ones.

## Common questions

### Do remote design roles pay less?

It depends on the company. Some pay one rate everywhere, others adjust by location. Look for the pay range in the listing, and ask early if there isn't one.

### Should my portfolio be public?

A public portfolio is easiest for a quick review. If your best work is under NDA, show a short, anonymised case study, or offer a walkthrough on a call.

### How do I show I'm good at remote work?

Mention distributed teams you worked with, the time zones you covered, and how you documented decisions. A well-written case study is itself proof.
